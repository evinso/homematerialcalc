## Depth guide by project

| Project | Minimum depth | Recommended depth |
|---------|--------------|-------------------|
| Decorative landscape path | 2" | 3" |
| Pedestrian walkway | 3" | 4" |
| Patio base (pavers) | 4" compacted + 1" sand | 5" + 1" sand |
| Patio base (concrete) | 4" | 6" |
| Residential driveway | 6" | 8" (base + surface) |
| Commercial driveway | 8" | 12" |
| French drain | Fill trench | 12"–18" deep trench |
| Around foundation | 3" slope away | 4" with fabric |
| Horse arena / paddock | 3" | 4"–6" |

## Why depth matters

### For driveways
Insufficient base depth is the leading cause of driveway ruts and potholes. The gravel compacts under traffic and displaces laterally. A minimum 4-inch compacted base absorbs load and resists movement.

### For patios and pavers
A 4-inch compacted gravel base prevents settling and frost heave. Skip this and your pavers will shift after the first winter freeze-thaw cycle.

### For drainage
Depth increases the volume of void space that stores and moves water. Shallow gravel in a drainage trench fills up faster and fails sooner.

## How to calculate gravel volume

**Volume (cu yd) = Length (ft) × Width (ft) × Depth (in) ÷ 324**

Or use the [Gravel Calculator](/calculator/gravel) — enter your dimensions and depth, and it outputs cubic yards, tons, and bags.

## Compaction factor

Gravel compacts 10–15% after installation. Order 10–15% extra for projects requiring compacted depth:
- Driveways: order 12% extra
- Patio bases: order 10% extra
- Paths: usually not necessary
