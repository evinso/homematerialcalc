## Sheets needed by room size (4×8 sheets, 8 ft ceiling, 12% waste, 1 door + 2 windows)

| Room size | Sheets (walls) | Sheets (ceiling) | **Total** |
|-----------|----------------|------------------|----------|
| 8 × 10 ft | 10 | 3 | **13** |
| 10 × 10 ft | 11 | 4 | **15** |
| 10 × 12 ft | 11 | 4 | **15** |
| 12 × 12 ft | 12 | 5 | **17** |
| 12 × 14 ft | 13 | 6 | **19** |
| 12 × 16 ft | 14 | 6 | **20** |
| 14 × 18 ft | 16 | 8 | **24** |
| 16 × 20 ft | 18 | 10 | **28** |

## Supplies per room

| Room size | Joint compound | Tape rolls (75 ft) | Screws (lbs) |
|-----------|---------------|-------------------|--------------|
| 10 × 12 ft | 3–4 gallons | 4 rolls | 3 lbs |
| 12 × 12 ft | 4–5 gallons | 5 rolls | 4 lbs |
| 12 × 16 ft | 5–6 gallons | 6 rolls | 5 lbs |

*Joint compound: 1 gallon per 100 sq ft for 3 coats. Tape: ~1 roll per 3 sheets.*

## 4×12 vs. 4×8 sheets

| Sheet size | Sheets per room (12×12) | Benefit |
|------------|------------------------|---------|
| 4 × 8 ft | 17 sheets | Easier to handle, most common |
| 4 × 12 ft | 12 sheets | Fewer seams, less finishing work |

4×12 sheets are harder to maneuver (especially on ceilings) but result in a smoother finish with fewer seams to tape.
